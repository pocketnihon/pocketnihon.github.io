Halal Food in Japan: Pork, Mirin and Hidden Alcohol

The main challenge is pork derivatives and alcohol in seasonings. Watch out for:

  • Pork: in gyōza, ramen broth (tonkotsu), many fried foods, sausages, ham and bacon
  • Lard (ラード): used in some fried rice, gyōza and ramen
  • Gelatin: often pork-derived, in desserts and sweets
  • Mirin (みりん): a sweet rice seasoning that usually contains alcohol, used in teriyaki, sukiyaki, simmered dishes and many sauces
  • Cooking sake (料理酒): used in marinades and simmered dishes
  • Meat that isn’t halal-certified: beef and chicken in ordinary restaurants are not halal

Good options:

  • Halal-certified restaurants, which are increasingly common in big cities and tourist areas
  • Muslim-friendly restaurants: some restaurants offer pork-free and alcohol-free menus even without full certification. Ask exactly what that means at each place.
  • Seafood and vegetable dishes, after confirming mirin and sake aren’t used. Note that regular soy sauce, many types of miso and other seasonings also contain a little alcohol, from fermentation or added as a preservative. If this matters to you, look for restaurants that use halal-certified seasonings.
  • Prayer rooms: some major airports, stations and shopping centres have prayer rooms. Ask at an information desk.
