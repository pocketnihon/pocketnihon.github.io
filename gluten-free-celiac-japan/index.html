Gluten-Free and Celiac in Japan: Soy Sauce, Soba and Safe Options

The main challenge is soy sauce and wheat flour in places you wouldn’t expect. Watch out for:

  • Soy sauce and everything made with it: teriyaki, ponzu, many dressings, marinades, yakitori tare
  • Miso: barley miso (麦味噌, mugi miso) contains barley. Rice miso (米味噌, kome miso) and soybean miso (豆味噌, mame miso) are normally made without wheat or barley, but always check.
  • Noodles: udon and ramen are wheat. Most soba also contains wheat flour (二八そば, ni-hachi soba, is about 20% wheat). Only 十割そば (jūwari soba) is 100% buckwheat, and it may still be boiled in shared water.
  • Fried food: tempura, karaage, tonkatsu and most fried food use wheat flour, and shared oil.
  • Curry: Japanese curry roux is thickened with wheat flour.
  • Barley tea (麦茶, mugicha): the free tea served in many restaurants is made from barley.
  • Imitation crab and fish cakes (kamaboko, chikuwa): may contain wheat flour or starch.

Good options:

  • Sashimi with your own wheat-free soy sauce or just salt
  • Plain grilled fish or meat with salt (塩, shio) instead of sauce. At yakitori restaurants, ask for shio rather than tare.
  • Rice dishes without sauce
  • Jūwari soba at shops that confirm no shared water, but note that the dipping sauce or broth (tsuyu) is made with regular soy sauce, which contains wheat. Ask for it without tsuyu or bring your own wheat-free soy sauce, and ask about flour on shared surfaces.
  • Carry your own wheat-free soy sauce. Small bottles of wheat-free soy sauce are sold in Japanese supermarkets; look for 小麦不使用 on the label.
