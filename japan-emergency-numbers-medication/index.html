Japan Emergency Numbers, Visitor Hotline and Bringing Medication

Emergency numbers

  • 119: ambulance and fire
  • 110: police
  • Japan Visitor Hotline: 050-3816-2787 (from abroad +81-50-3816-2787). Run by the Japan National Tourism Organization, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, in English, Chinese, Korean and Japanese. It helps in emergencies such as illness, accidents and disasters.

Calling 119 or 110 is free. Say:

  • 救急車を呼んでください。(Kyūkyūsha o yonde kudasai.) “Please call an ambulance.”
  • アレルギーの症状が出ています。(Arerugī no shōjō ga dete imasu.) “I’m having an allergic reaction.”

Bringing your medication

Japan allows you to bring prescription medicine for your own use without special paperwork in many cases, generally up to a one-month supply. For injectable medicine, the personal-use allowance covers pre-filled syringes and self-injection kits such as adrenaline auto-injectors. For more than a one-month supply, you need to apply for an import confirmation in advance. Some common medicines, including certain stimulants and strong painkillers, are restricted or banned in Japan.

Rules change, so check the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) guidance or your nearest Japanese embassy before you travel. Carry a letter from your doctor listing your medication and your allergy, and keep your medication in its original packaging with the pharmacy label.

Pharmacies

Pharmacies (薬局, yakkyoku, or ドラッグストア, doraggu sutoa) are easy to find in cities. Staff can help with over-the-counter medicines, though not all speak English. Show the name of the active ingredient rather than a brand name, as brands differ between countries.
