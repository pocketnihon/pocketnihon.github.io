How to Read Japanese Food Labels for Allergens (Updated 2026)

Packaged food in Japan has a standard label. To check for allergens:

  1. Find 原材料名 (genzairyō-mei), the list of ingredients. Ingredients are listed in order of quantity.
  2. Look for 「一部に＿＿を含む」 (ichibu ni ＿＿ o fukumu), “contains ＿＿ in part.” This allergen summary is usually at the end of the ingredient list, in brackets. For example: （一部に小麦・大豆を含む） means “contains wheat and soy.”
  3. Some products list allergens next to each ingredient instead of all together, for example: しょうゆ（小麦・大豆を含む）, “soy sauce (contains wheat and soy).”
  4. Look for a separate allergen box on some products, a small table with marked icons.

What the rules require:

  • Packaged food must declare 9 mandatory allergens (特定原材料): shrimp, crab, walnut, wheat, buckwheat, egg, milk, peanut and cashew.
  • Cashew became mandatory in April 2026. Products manufactured or imported before April 2028 may still use the old labelling, so cashew can be missing from some packages during this transition.
  • Walnut was added in March 2023, with a transition period that ended on 31 March 2025, so all labels must now show it.
  • Many other allergens, including sesame, soy, salmon, mackerel, gelatin, pork, chicken and beef, are only recommended for labelling, not required. If they aren’t listed, that doesn’t prove the food is free of them.
