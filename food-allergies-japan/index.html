Food Allergies in Japan: Where Allergens Hide in Japanese Food

Japan’s mandatory allergen list (for packaged food) is a good guide to the ingredients Japanese cooks use most:

Allergen Japanese Where it hides
Shrimp えび Tempura, kakiage, rice crackers (ebisen), dried shrimp (sakura ebi), stock
Crab かに Imitation crab (kanikama) often contains crab extract; stock
Walnut くるみ Walnut-dressed vegetables (くるみ和え), sweets, bread
Wheat 小麦 Soy sauce, noodles, batter, curry, many sauces
Buckwheat そば Soba, soba tea, soba boro cookies, galettes; shared boiling water
Egg 卵 Mayonnaise, tempura batter, fish cakes, binders in hamburg steak and tsukune, rolled omelette, sweets
Milk 乳 Butter, cream, cheese, white sauce, curry and stew roux, bread, Western-style sweets
Peanut 落花生 Sauces, dressings, sweets, peanut oil
Cashew カシューナッツ Sweets, stir-fries, snack mixes

Sesame (ごま) deserves a special mention: it isn’t on the mandatory list, but it’s everywhere in Japanese food: sesame oil, sesame sauce (gomadare), ground sesame on vegetables, furikake rice seasoning and chili oil (rāyu). Fish isn’t on the mandatory list either, but fish dashi and bonito flakes are in a huge share of dishes.

If you have a severe allergy:

  • Show the card every time, even at a place you’ve been before.
  • Ask about shared oil and cookware (the cards include this).
  • Avoid dishes with complex sauces and broths.
  • Carry your medication at all times (see our emergency guide).
