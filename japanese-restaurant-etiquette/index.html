Japanese Restaurant Etiquette: 12 Things to Know Before You Eat Out in Japan

Eating out is one of the best parts of a trip to Japan. Nobody expects visitors to know every rule, but a few basics will make you feel more comfortable, and staff will appreciate the effort.

Arriving

1. Wait to be seated. At most restaurants, a staff member will greet you with irasshaimase (“welcome”) and ask how many people are in your group. You don’t need to reply to irasshaimase; just hold up fingers for the number of people.

2. Take your shoes off if you see a raised floor. In some restaurants you sit on tatami mats. If there’s a step up and shoes lined up at the edge, take yours off before stepping up.

3. Use the oshibori for your hands only. The hot or cold wet towel you’re given is for wiping your hands before eating. Avoid using it on your face, at least in nicer restaurants.

Ordering

4. Call staff with “sumimasen”. In casual restaurants and izakaya, staff often won’t come back to your table unless you call them. Raise your hand and say sumimasen (“excuse me”). Many restaurants also have a call button on the table, and many chains now use a tablet or a QR code for ordering.

5. Water and tea are usually free. In most restaurants, water or green tea is served free of charge, and you can ask for refills.

6. Ask about ingredients if you need to. Japanese dishes often contain fish stock, soy sauce (which contains wheat) and other hidden ingredients. If you have an allergy or follow a special diet, ask before you order. See our guide on how to ask about ingredients.

Eating

7. Say “itadakimasu” before you eat. It means something like “I humbly receive” and is said before every meal. It’s a nice touch, but no one will mind if you forget.

8. Chopstick rules to remember. - Don’t stick your chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice. This is linked to funeral rituals. - Don’t pass food from your chopsticks to someone else’s chopsticks. This is also a funeral custom. - When you’re not using them, rest your chopsticks on the chopstick rest (hashioki). If there isn’t one, fold the paper chopstick wrapper into a rest. - When taking food from a shared plate, use the serving chopsticks (toribashi) if they’re provided, or ask for some.

9. Slurping noodles is fine. At ramen, soba and udon shops, slurping is normal and even helps cool the noodles. You can also drink the soup straight from the bowl.

10. Don’t walk while eating. Eating while walking down the street is generally frowned upon in Japan. If you buy street food or a convenience store snack, eat it near the stall or at the shop’s eat-in space.

Paying

11. Pay at the register, not at the table. At most casual restaurants, take the bill (often left face-down on your table) to the register by the exit. At izakaya and upscale restaurants you may pay at the table instead. At some ramen shops and casual restaurants, you buy a meal ticket from a machine (shokkenki) before you sit down.

12. Don’t tip. Tipping isn’t part of Japanese culture. See Tipping in Japan for the extra charges you might see on your bill instead.

When you leave, saying gochisōsama deshita (“thank you for the meal”) is a lovely way to finish.
