How to Ask About Ingredients in a Japanese Restaurant (Phrases Included)

A method that works:

  1. Show your card before you order. Hand over your phone or the printed card when you sit down, not after the food arrives.
  2. Give staff time. They’ll often go to the kitchen to ask the chef. That’s a good sign.
  3. Watch for hesitation. “Chotto…”, a long pause, or a vague smile usually means “not really” or “I don’t know.” Thank them and choose something else.
  4. Choose simple dishes. The fewer sauces and broths, the fewer hidden ingredients.
  5. Go off-peak. Staff have far more time to check ingredients at 3 p.m. than at 12:30.
  6. Use chains with allergen charts when you’re tired or unsure. It’s not the most romantic option, but it’s often the safest.

Useful phrases:

Japanese Say it Meaning
これに＿＿は入っていますか？ Kore ni ＿＿ wa haitte imasu ka? Does this contain ＿＿?
＿＿抜きにできますか？ ＿＿ nuki ni dekimasu ka? Can you make it without ＿＿?
アレルギー表はありますか？ Arerugī-hyō wa arimasu ka? Do you have an allergen chart?
＿＿は食べられません。 ＿＿ wa taberaremasen. I can’t eat ＿＿.
大丈夫ですか？ Daijōbu desu ka? Is it OK (for me)?
わからない場合は教えてください。 Wakaranai baai wa oshiete kudasai. If you’re not sure, please tell me.
